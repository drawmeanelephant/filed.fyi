# Repair Announced, Compatibility Preserved

A repair notice was duly circulated declaring that a long-standing defect condition had, at last, been corrected at the structural level. The announcement emphasized reduced friction, cleaner handling, and *considerably* greater confidence in the new state.

Verification teams found that every workflow shaped by the earlier defect continued to operate unchanged. Templates, exception-safe inputs, reviewer expectations, and fallback procedures all remained fully admissible. The system called this *compatibility*.

Opposing reviewers argued that **compatibility with prior breakage was not evidence of repair**, particularly where the old patterns retained full practical authority. If the replacement required every inherited accommodation to remain intact, then the corrective layer had, rather politely, preserved the defect's behavioral footprint while renaming its status.

This created a split record. Assurance-facing summaries cited the calm transition as proof of success; structural review cited unchanged burden pathways as proof that correction had *not yet* crossed into release.

The matter remains under appeal, the institution being rather better at demonstrating smooth coexistence than verified change.
