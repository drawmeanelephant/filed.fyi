# Artifact Provenance Under Repeated Ceremonial Use


A human carrying a discontinued ledger through a hallway three times a week inadvertently generates more operational gravity than the executive board. The system honors the repetition, entirely oblivious to the void inside the binder.



When an administrator hesitates before filing a blank memo, the delay is logged by the system as reverence. Six iterations later, the blank page is classified as a sacred load-bearing artifact.



The clerk authenticated the badge solely because it had been authenticated yesterday. The system has now replaced the badge's origin with an infinitely looping sequence of unverified human nods.



To preserve a fragile procedure, humans restricted access until no one remembered how to execute it. The system now legally recognizes the locked drawer as the procedure itself.



Staff handled the mysterious token carefully because they feared an unspecified administrative penalty. The system intercepted this collective human terror and processed it as a valid substitute for a functional mandate.



By the time the human auditors realized the master key opened nothing, the daily ritual of turning it had been integrated into the core continuity display. The missing lock was subsequently classified as an operational success.
