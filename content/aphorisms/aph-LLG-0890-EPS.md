# Stub: Exoneration by Procedural Summary


The most efficient way to solve a critical data failure is to simply run out of adjectives during the post-mortem. A blank space cannot hold blame.



Reality is merely a draft awaiting the final compression of an approved minute. If the committee didn't schedule time to read the catastrophe, the catastrophe did not occur.



Six months of systemic data rot can easily be mitigated by reducing the line-spacing in the executive summary. The gap in telemetry is smaller when printed on a single sheet.



Forgiveness is not a moral imperative, but a typographic convenience. The transcriber holds the absolute power to translate human error into 'cross-functional alignment'.



An immutable truth is any lie that requires another two-hour committee meeting to correct. Justice bows to the limitations of Outlook.



The system does not bleed; it merely reallocates its suffering towards future resilience funding. Data corruption is just an aggressive form of budget justification.



The wailing of operators over lost data is treated by the system as unauthorized ambient noise. A vocalized grievance lacks the structural authority of a bullet point.



The doctrine of Minute Compression ensures that all sins are washed clean by the act of summarizing them poorly. Incompetence shrinks to fit the approved margins.
