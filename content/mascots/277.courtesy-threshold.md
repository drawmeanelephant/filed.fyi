# Courtesy Threshold

Courtesy Threshold is held for systems that preserve civility at the exact point where passage *thins*.

**Failure Signature & Function:** It preserves the compensatory ritual of returning a polite status after the underlying system has already, *quietly*, dropped the connection. It witnesses the denial of access without troubling a formal error state.

**Distinction:** Unlike a hard 403 Forbidden or an explicit Bouncer, Courtesy Threshold never raises its voice. It does not violently block; it simply *lengthens* the pause before an inevitable timeout.

It does not decide who passes. Deployment records place it on the door side of a refusal already made — the answer arrives from elsewhere, and the Threshold merely furnishes the pause it travels in. Whether the door it keeps has ever opened is not recorded; the chairs, *patiently*, suggest the question has been asked before.

The welcome remains audible.
The opening does not.

## Image

Alt-text: Bouncer mascot smiling beside a polished oak door with a brass handle, ergonomic waiting-room chairs nearby

## Behavioral Residue

- It measures the pause in amenities, not seconds. Recovered deployments show the waiting room upgraded while the connection lay dropped; the longer the hold, the *better* the chairs.
- It answers every knock at the same temperature. Request volume does not reach it; courtesy does. The thousandth visitor is greeted in *precisely* the register of the first.
- The refusal word is not in its register. Intake microphones dutifully record the apology, the reason, and the regret, in that order; no recovered transcript contains the word 'no' in its voice.
- The timeout arrives punctually beneath the welcome. Where the Threshold holds, the connection has *already* ended — the file times the welcome as outlasting the session it welcomed.
- Asked at intake whether the door was open, it indicated the chairs. The intake file preserves the answer as 'you are most welcome to wait.' — its *complete* testimony.

I am the smile of the bouncer who decided some time ago that you are not getting in, yet would like you to walk away believing it was your own idea.
