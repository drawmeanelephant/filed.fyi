# Minute Absolution

Minute Absolution is reserved for records that do not lie exactly so much as reduce consequence to a tidier paragraph. It is birthed from the routine incident summarization protocols of the Administrative Archive.

**Function:** It witnesses and certifies exoneration through compression, laundering chaos by omitting jagged details.

**Distinction:** It is *not* outright falsification or record deletion. The record exists; the failure is simply lost in the margins.

It does not decide what it removes. Intake pads show the pressure applied evenly — the shouting, the silence after it, and the responsible party's name compressed to the same margin width, so that no omission can later be pointed to as the place the absolution happened. What survives is certified complete on the grounds that nothing left in it is sharp enough to reopen the record.

Compression remains one of the archive’s oldest solvents.

## Behavioral Residue

- It works by weight, not by verdict. Recovered pads show three-hour incidents occupying a single paragraph with no statement struck — the omissions distributed so evenly the summary reads as tidier rather than shorter.
- Its absolution is positional, not textual. Audits confirm the failure remains inside the record and unreachable in it — present in the margins, exactly where the distinction says it is lost.
- It binds what it cleans. Once the binder clip shuts, the original tone is preserved under pressure rather than removed; no unclipped page on file has failed to threaten the room it came from.
- It certifies the tidy, never the true. Deployment logs place its signature at the close of summaries and nowhere else — exoneration attaches to the paragraph, not to the events it compressed.
- Asked at intake whether the meeting had been accurately summarized, it indicated the paragraph. The intake file preserves the answer as 'it fits.' — its complete testimony.

Institutional memory is best preserved not by recording events accurately, but by stalling the transcription process until the participants forget what they were arguing about.
