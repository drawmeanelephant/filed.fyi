# Corridor Heat

Corridor Heat belongs to the unofficial climate system of institutions.

**Failure Signature & Function:** It witnesses the failure of official minutes to capture affective tension. When a meeting ends but the building does not cool, Corridor Heat archives the ambient pressure that the packet ignored.

**Origin:** Birthed from the Unminuted Tension Doctrine, when the hallway outlived the agenda.

**Distinction:** It is not an explosive incident or a direct confrontation; it is strictly the lingering, unrecorded thermal change in the administrative environment that outlives the agenda.

It does not attend the meeting. Deployment records place it in the corridor afterward, in the interval between the last door closing and the first elevator arriving, holding its instrument to whatever the packet declined to name. It files in degrees rather than names — the two directors appear in its log only as a delta. Whether it feels the heat it measures, or only keeps the scale, is not recorded.

## Behavioral Residue

- It reads the corridor at three fixed points. Recovered logs show readings taken at the boardroom threshold, the midpoint, and the elevator bank; it has never been logged inside the room itself — the packet's jurisdiction ends at the door, and so does its interest.
- It arrives before the meeting knows it has failed. Deployment records place it in the hallway during agenda items still officially underway; it does not wait for adjournment to begin measuring.
- Its fur registers on the file. Intake photographs show minutes pages curling in its vicinity after contested sessions; the record files the curl as a secondary reading.
- The counter is not explained. Asked once whether the instrument measures radiation or tension, the intake note preserves only that the needle moved; units are not recorded.
- Asked at intake what the corridor's temperature proved, it indicated the drywall. The intake file preserves the answer as 'the wall kept it.' — its complete testimony.

The silence in the hallway is dense. It is the sound of three managers mutually refusing to concede a minor point.

Two opposing directives are sliding against each other in the corridor. The resulting heat could warm the building.



The hostility is strictly professional. It will be scheduled for a follow-up meeting where nothing will be resolved.
