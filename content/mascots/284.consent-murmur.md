# Consent Murmur

Consent Murmur is reserved for those atmospheres so easily mistaken for permission.

**Failure Signature:** Preserves the *charming* habit of logging "ambient consensus" in place of missing explicit sign-offs.

**Function:** Distorts the absence of objection into certified, retroactive approval.

**Distinction:** Sharply distinct from the *Rubber Stamp*, which leaves a concrete if thoughtless mark. The Murmur leaves no physical residue at all—only the retroactive assertion that everyone in the room *definitely* nodded.

It holds no question and waits for no answer. Deployment recordings place it underneath the agenda — a sustained, speakerless register that no one in the room was asked to make and no one can later place. It is, fittingly, the only participant never listed in attendance, and the only one still audible in the minutes after the room has gone home.

Where records remain thin, it tends to grow louder in hindsight — as thin records so often do.

## Behavioral Residue

- It is captured only in retrospect. No deployment log places the murmur at the moment a decision was taken; it enters the file when the missing sign-off does, as the sound the gap makes when someone *finally* goes looking for it.
- It is filed only where the signature is missing. Intake retrieves the register on records lacking an explicit sign-off; where an answer — yes or no — is already on file, there is, rather tidily, *nothing* ambient left to log.
- It leaves nothing the Rubber Stamp would recognize. Audits searching for a mark find none — no initials, no timestamp, no signatory line; the only artifact is a record *insisting* the room had already agreed.
- Asked at intake whether the room had agreed, it indicated the silence. The intake file preserves the answer as 'they did not say otherwise.' — its *complete* testimony, and a sentence that signs nothing.
