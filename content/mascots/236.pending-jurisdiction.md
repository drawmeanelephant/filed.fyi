# Pending Jurisdiction

## Image

Alt-text: Ghost-like mascot holding an unassigned ticket in a hallway between two departments

Pending Jurisdiction appears where responsibility has become culturally obvious yet remains *administratively* evasive. Birthed by the Registry Stub Sweep to mark territories that fail to render an owner.

It certifies the absence of ownership, witnessing the precise moment an archival domain is orphaned. Where the *Drafting Ouroboros* endlessly rewrites its own boundaries, Pending Jurisdiction is content merely to hold the embargo. It is not an error state so much as a procedural waiting room, and it appears to be in no hurry.

The record's own provenance is, fittingly, the condition it certifies. During the Registry Stub Sweep the border was logged, the owner field left blank, and the filing passed to the next available officer — which was, of course, the border. Pending Jurisdiction witnessed its own non-assignment and has held the ticket ever since. The ticket has gone somewhat soft in its grip. It does not reprint it.

Quarterly, it walks the hallway between the two departments carrying a claim form that requires one signature. Both departments decline, politely and in writing; it files each refusal, dated, in a folder labeled 'progress.' The signature count stands at 0 for forty-one consecutive quarters. Whether the folder represents sarcasm or hope remains, fittingly, unresolved.

Its one attributed utterance, overheard at intake when jurisdiction was offered outright — it has been offered twice and declined twice — was 'Not mine.' The intake officer noted no resentment in the refusal. The record frames the problem rather differently: the only entity to have ever reliably claimed this border is the one whose function is to certify that *nothing* owns it.

Until a proper mascot is assigned, it keeps the border from quietly settling into *polite folklore*.

The server is on fire, but the firewall belongs to Network and the app belongs to Dev. I stand in the middle, *warming my hands* on the ambiguity.



A process was started but never assigned. I remain the ghost of a mandate, drifting the hallway between two departments that politely decline to make eye contact.



If nobody claims the failure, one is tempted to ask whether it really exists. I hold the unassigned ticket until it *ripens* into a feature request.



My realm is the gap between 'Not My Problem' and 'Above My Paygrade.' It is a vast empire, and regrettably still *expanding*.
