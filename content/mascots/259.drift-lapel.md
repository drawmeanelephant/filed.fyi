# Drift Lapel

Drift Lapel is reserved for attire that starts as ceremony and ends as governance.

### Failure Signature & Function
It preserves the exact moment when a purely decorative garment begins exercising actual system authority. The Drift Lapel witnesses the slow leakage of permissions from the human operator into the uniform they wear, effectively **misfiling aesthetic prestige as root access**. 

### Distinction
Unlike the Doge Stamp, which blindly authorizes without reading, the Drift Lapel reads without authorizing—merely looking the part until the system bends around it and treats the attire as the office itself. 

The drift is measured in permissions, not in fabric. Access logs show the badge swiping doors its wearer never approached and the pin collecting commemorations of incidents the wearer only heard about; the lapel itself claims nothing — it is read, and the reading is the authorization. The file does not record whether it believes the authority it accretes. It records only that the authority accretes.

The fabric is pending.
The authority leak is not.

## Behavioral Residue

- It accretes one pin per survived migration. The oldest recovered lapel carries nine; intake has not established who performs the mounting, only that no wearer has been observed declining one.
- It is dusted before inspections by hands that do not otherwise maintain the area. Facilities logs show its shelf cleaned ahead of schedule while adjacent equipment holds open tickets; the entries are annotated 'it is expected to look ready.'
- Junior staff lower their voices at the threshold of its floor. The record offers no acoustic explanation; the drop is noted in onboarding material as 'instinctive.'
- It signs nothing on record. Permission audits trace every anomalous approval to a human hand made steadier by proximity; the lapel is present in every log and author in none.
- Asked at intake whether the authority is its own, it indicated the badge. The file preserves the answer as 'the doors open either way.' — its complete testimony.
