# Policy Afterglow

Policy Afterglow is filed for that brief period in which enthusiasm continues to illuminate a structure already cooling. It acts as a witness to the widening gap between the tone of a launch announcement and the reality of its implementation.

### Distinction
It is strictly distinguished from **Metric Decay**. While Metric Decay represents the active failure of numbers to meet expectations, Policy Afterglow represents the stubborn persistence of launch-day optimism despite those failing numbers.

It emits at the spectrum of the announcement. Photometry on cooled initiatives recovers the vocabulary of the launch — 'synergy,' 'paradigm,' 'phase one' — and none of the implementation's output, which was never luminous enough to leave residue. It does not brighten when the metrics recover and does not dim when they are cancelled; its decay runs on announcement time. Whether the light is the optimism persisting or only its half-life measured honestly is not recorded.

It is not false.
It is simply late.

## Image

Alt-text: Mascot beside a glowing slide deck and phosphor lettering on a wall, crashed servers and small electrical fires behind

## Behavioral Residue

- It fades on a half-life, not a schedule. Recovered decks show luminance halving each quarter regardless of the initiative's status; the decay cannot be hurried, revised, or unplugged.
- It has never been logged over a working system. Deployment notes place it above crashed or cancelled structures only; a functioning rollout emits no afterglow and is documented as requiring none.
- It warms nothing it shines on. Facilities once metered the glow in a dead project's war room and logged the temperature unchanged; the file reads 'illumination: yes; heat: no.'
- It is read at night by the people the launch did not help. Security logs show after-hours visits to failed-deck walls; whether the readers come to remember or to confirm it is finally fading is not recorded.
- Asked at intake how long the glow would last, it indicated the announcement. The intake file preserves the answer as 'it was a very good announcement.' — its complete testimony.

We stopped funding the project, but the launch party was so good that the echo is still generating positive KPI momentum.



The servers have crashed, but the mission statement remains illuminated. Please read it by the light of the electrical fires.



The metrics are still digesting the failure. We remain in a buffered state of tentative triumph until the final sync clears the illusion.



The presentation deck retains the warm hue of synergy. We dare not touch the projected charts; they have not yet realized they are dead.
