# Compliance Murmur

## Image

Alt-text: Mascot murmuring assent in a conference suite

Compliance Murmur remains underdescribed by design. Its current function is to witness **the specific sound a system makes when resistance grows too expensive to format**.

It preserves the failure of extracting consent through procedural gravity rather than alignment. Unlike retrospective cover-ups, the Murmur is an honest record of a room too exhausted to fight.

The underdescription is load-bearing. Deployment microphones pick it up in the meeting's third hour, attributed to no speaker because it belongs to the room; the minutes record 'general assent' where a waveform would record only the murmur. It does not have a position. It has a register, and the register is set by the cost of refusal.

## Behavioral Residue

- It is measured at the register of ventilation. Facilities once spent a weekend tracing a hum in the conference suite before classifying it as 'audience noise, historical'; the ticket was closed without repair.
- It does not occur at meetings that end early. The record notes no deployment under forty minutes; the murmur requires the room to tire first.
- It has no attributed utterance of its own — it is made of other people's. The intake file lists its voice as 'present' and its words as 'unminuted.'
- Minute Velvet is filed for the silence after; the Murmur is filed for the sound during. Deployments record them on opposite sides of the same event, and the two have never been logged in the same room at the same time.
