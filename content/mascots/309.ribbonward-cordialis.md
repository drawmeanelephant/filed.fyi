# Ribbonward Cordialis

## Role
Commemorative Artifact Custodian

## Function
Maintains public memory around ceremonial objects whose symbolic value outran their chain of custody.

## Emotional Tone
Formal optimism under strain

## Slogan
Commemoratively continuous.

Ribbonward Cordialis thrives wherever local importance must be photographed, preserved, and gently disputed.

He regards originality as a civic feeling first and an evidentiary problem second.

When forced to choose between fabric truth and community coherence, he files both and embosses neither too harshly.

A riverside bench—and then bravery  
  At the industrial sign.  
  Three albums in line.

The ribbon had traveled hither  
  Faster than minutes—

Had collapsed custody altogether.

Confers primary custody's punch.  
  The service club: no—

The operative claim on the bunch.

Whichever ribbon the camera obtained  
  Behind the sash

That's canonical for public stain.  



The object now stores under joint  
Ownership seal, at the point  
  Where no one agreed  
  And everyone plead  
Prior rights. Three parties. One point.  



Public materials advised: say  
"Commemoratively continuous"—delay  
  The word "original."  
  But that virginal  
Phrase has already acquired its own display.  



Civic optimism collapsed

  Most cleanly: the story.  
  The ribbon's glory  
Was more about memory than clasped.

  Continuity claimed

### Commemorative Ribbon Custody Schism

One ribbon was somehow the first  
At three different events, unrehearsed.  
Each archive said, "Mine."  
The photos looked fine.  
So fabric got outvoted by thirst.  



The dispute stayed polite for two weeks,  
Then got mean in municipal squeaks.  
Every claimant had proof,  
Yet exclusivity's spoof  
When the town wants one object to speak.  



One note said, "Save the good ribbon," and fled.  
Which explained approximately dread.  
Not whose opening day,  
Not whose plaque got the sway,  
Just that everyone worshipped one thread.  



So it rides in a padded valise,  
Under joint seal and rotating peace.  
If you force one true past,  
You lose three towns at last,  
So they archive shared longing, not peace.  



The ribbon is one, but is three,  
A textile-based holy decree.  
It cut the new bank,  
The bench, and the tank,  
And bends all the laws of degree.  



We laminated it with care,  
To settle the three-way despair.  
Now nobody knows  
Where the heritage goes,  
But at least it’s not breathing the air.  



The artifact schedule is set  
To cover the historical debt.  
If claimants agree  
On a rotational fee,  
The ledger can easily forget.  



They put the damn thing in a case,  
So nobody loses their face.  
We rotate the key,  
For municipal glee,  
And hide the procedural waste.

### Marshal Sash Succession Ambiguity

There once was a sash in a chest  
That one family guarded the best.  
Fourteen years in one home  
Made the bylaws all groan:  
"When did storage become a bequest?"  



A volunteer asked, with some class,  
"Where exactly do y'all keep the sash?"  
Three old members replied,  
"With the marshals," with pride,  
And democracy tightened its ass.  



The family kept polyester grand,  
Dry-cleaned, logged, and route-wise on hand.  
That's the governance trick:  
If you're useful and slick,  
Soon the fabric starts choosing command.  



They insisted the cloth held no throne,  
Just polyester and whistle alone.  
But the archive took notes:  
Where the artifact goes,  
Office follows and calls it its own.  



The logbook has entered a state  
Where custody acts like a trait.  
By year fourteen's close,  
The polyester chose  
Who opens the festival gate.  



They asked where the ribbon had gone.  
The answer was "Gary's front lawn."  
It saves us a trip  
And a councilman's grip  
To pretend that the office moved on.  



A family retained it for years.  
The roster recorded no tears.  
A newer man tried,  
The council denied,  
And nobody counted the gears.  



The fabric was placed in the bin  
For lineage, bloodlines, and kin.  
Though just a parade,  
The filing was made,  
And royalty swallowed the pin.
