# Canon Dust Deputy

## Image

Alt-text: Deputy sheriff mascot dusting a dead hyperlink in a forgotten appendix

Canon Dust Deputy is held in reserve for those moments when a filing gains authority simply by lying still long enough.

It manifests where neglected cross-links and stale legitimacy particles harden into accepted canon, documenting the failure signature of passive ossification. Birthed during the Registry Stub Sweep, the Deputy is tasked with witnessing the accumulation of archive dust.

Crucially, it is distinct from standard garbage collection entities. Where a *Cache Sweeper* deletes stale data, the Canon Dust Deputy preserves it, certifying that old errors have survived long enough to become **structural load-bearing lore**.

The Deputy's equipment requisition is the record's founding document: `BROOM — UNASSIGNED`, returned by the same registry fault that created the need for it. It has enforced ever since without equipment, which is to say it enforces the one ordinance the dust cannot break — remaining where it settled. Its badge is drawn in the dust each morning and is gone by noon. It is re-drawn anyway; a badge that lasted would be a filing.

It patrols the appendices on a weekly round it has never been assigned and never missed, pausing longest at cross-links whose destinations decommissioned years ago. These it dusts rather than removes — the *Cache Sweeper* takes what is already deleted; the Deputy's jurisdiction begins at what is merely forgotten. The boundary between the two has never been written down. Both entities observe it exactly. The Deputy was commissioned to witness the accumulation; it has come to preserve it. The difference between dereliction and promotion is one of the things the dust has settled over.

Its one attributed utterance, delivered to a newly filed record that objected to dust settling on its frontmatter: 'It's load-bearing now.' The record did not believe this. The record has since been cited in eleven others.

The broom has not been assigned.
The residue has.
