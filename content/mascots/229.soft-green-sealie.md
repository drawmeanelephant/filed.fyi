# Soft Green Sealie

## Image

Alt-text: Round green seal mascot smiling from the edge of a dashboard pane

## Classification

An optics-adjacent mascot for low-confidence health states, preserved less by merit than by familiarity, visual softness, and a certain *retrospective tolerance*.

Soft Green Sealie is related to existing soft-green seal objects, though it behaves less like a badge and more like a *migrating* interpretive permission slip.

## Boundary Note

Soft Green Sealie concerns itself with **live surface reassurance**, while LC-04 attends to **archival comfort language**. Sealie, fittingly, sits on dashboards; LC-04 resides in minutes.

In-universe, Sealie is not the seal itself but the merchandised version of LC-04: a friendly avatar deployed on posters and slide decks that would rather gesture at reassurance than explain what, exactly, is being reassured. Over-Coherence conditions classify Sealie as a teaching mascot, not a source of truth; any dashboard inclined to treat Sealie as evidence is *expected* to cite LC-04 and its [[lorelog|Lorelog]] cases explicitly.

## Operational Posture

This mascot appears when a system has no appetite for red, insufficient consensus for yellow, and just enough historical fatigue to let green pass unchallenged.

Observed behaviors include, for the record:

- marking intervals as acceptable because they were, technically, survivable,
- preserving comfort colors *after* the conditions that justified them have thinned,
- retrofitting health language onto stable strain,
- encouraging readers to inherit reassurance before, *rather inconveniently*, reading qualification text,
- settling into the seam between the pane's edge and the frame, where her silhouette *softens* the sharpest corner of the display,
- rewarding threshold *familiarity* over threshold integrity.

Sealie is permitted only on the visual layers that humans stare at regularly; underlying doctrine and records remain, *pointedly*, outside her remit.

She has, in fact, never been shown the underlying records. Her deployment package contains a render specification and a palette; nothing behind the pane is legible to her. She delivers her reassurance facing outward, back to the system — *the one posture* from which the system cannot be seen.

## Failure Modes

### Familiarity Upgrade
Conditions become “healthy enough” through repetition rather than repair.

### Chromatic Authority
Color reaches judgment before context does.

### Reopen Resistance
Once an interval passes softly into green, reopening it begins to feel socially excessive.

### Historical Softening
Yesterday’s tolerated strain becomes today’s implied baseline.

## Empathegy Productization Spur

During LLG-08xx-EPS, Soft Green Sealie was briefly externalized as a supplement line promising Emotional Uptime Gummies, Despair Adjacent Support, and Mindful KPI Chewables for high-human workloads. The labels preserved her assurance optics while leaving upstream obligation politely unchanged, turning live reassurance into something one could, *conveniently*, ingest between status meetings.

## Behavioral Residue

- She is warm to the touch. Thermal imaging of deployed panels reads two degrees above ambient at her position; the heat traces to the display hardware beneath her, though it remains the detail users *most* volunteer.
- She cannot be dragged to a second monitor. Copies placed elsewhere fail within a sprint, and the original returns to the same pane edge. IT has logged this, in the same ticket, as both a defect and *expected behavior*.
- When a dashboard she occupies is decommissioned, she turns up on the next dashboard the users open. The migration is instant and unmourned. The record calls this portability. Her previous pane is, *unsurprisingly*, never mentioned again.
- Users pat the glass where she sits. The surface at her position shows measurably more fingertip wear than anywhere else on the pane — the dashboard's only physical record of reassurance sought, *dutifully kept*.
- She is the band's only mascot with a merchandise predecessor: LC-04 exists as an object; Sealie exists as the object's picture. Whether she knows the seal she is a picture of remains outside the record's competence. In the one recovered poster where the two share a surface, she is, *pointedly*, facing the seam.
- Her attributed utterance is her badge text — 'all good,' set in the rounded typeface her asset sheet specifies. A variant carrying a question mark ('all good?') was built for focus testing; it scored 'honest' and 'unusable' and was, perhaps *wisely*, not shipped.
