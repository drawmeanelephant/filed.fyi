# Attestation Mole

## Image

Alt-text: Blind mole mascot stamping a seal on a form underground

Attestation Mole remains mostly inferential, a subsurface verifier responsible for blind certainties beneath the visible form layer.

### System Function & Jurisdictional Classification
Operating under the Core Attestation Layer, the Mole exists to stamp and seal records where the actual chain of evidence is missing or obscured. It **certifies the unseeable**, witnessing processes that execute without logs.

### Causal Anchoring & Failure Signature
Birthed from the Registry Stub Sweep, the Mole manifested when the archive realized it had thousands of signatures that appeared cleaner than the processes that produced them. The recurring breakdown it preserves is the system's need to blindly trust hidden processes when visible telemetry fails.

### Distinction
The Attestation Mole is distinct from the Validation Hound; where the Hound sniffs out explicit structural errors in broad daylight, the Mole blindly rubber-stamps structural voids in the dark, asserting that the unseen foundation must be sound for the surface to hold.

The Mole itself has never been photographed. Deployment evidence is the stamp — found on the undersides of forms, in margins the surface reader never reaches, on carbon copies the file no longer admits having. Its certainty is structural: the surface held, therefore the foundation was sound; the reasoning does not run the other way. The record does not say how many moles there are.

## Behavioral Residue

- Its tunnels are mapped by negation. Engineers locate its runs by the forms that arrive pre-stamped; a run that produces a question instead of a seal is reclassified as a fault and routed to the daylight layer.
- It stamps in the dark without error. Recovered forms show seal placement accurate to the signature line even where the form's layout changed mid-run; it does not read the form. It knows where lines go.
- Its attributed utterance is pressure. The intake file lists no voice; the only sound it is documented making is the seal's give under the stamp, which deployment microphones render as 'the form agreeing.'
- The Validation Hound works the daylight layer. Deployments place the two on the same file within the same week and never at the same table; the Mole certifies what the Hound's report never reached.
- One recovered deployment shows a form with the seal struck twice, once over the other — the only documented hesitation on record. Both stamps are valid.
