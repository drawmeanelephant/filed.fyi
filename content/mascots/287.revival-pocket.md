# Revival Pocket

Revival Pocket is reserved for recoveries too local to trust and too hopeful to dismiss. It serves as a witness to the false starts of system restoration, certifying the exact spatial and temporal bounds where a doomed process *temporarily* drew breath.

Born of the localized anomalies documented in incident [[lorelog/LLG-0398-CCO|LLG-0398-CCO]], the pocket manifests when a deprecated system inexplicably works in a single room, then promptly fails the moment it is asked to generalize. 

Crucially, it is not a **Resurrection Node**. A Resurrection Node implies a permanent return to baseline functionality; a Revival Pocket is a fragile, localized bubble patiently awaiting collapse. The archive keeps them shelved near relaunch residue, for reasons that should not require explaining.

It is measured before it is believed. Deployment records show the tape going down while the terminal still breathes — a perimeter surveyed around a recovery the surveyors expect, politely, to lose. The pocket does not claim the machine is fixed; it claims only that here, briefly, the machine can be stood next to. The boundary outlives the miracle by exactly the width of the tape.

## Behavioral Residue

- It is cordoned while still warm. Intake files show the floor marked off during the anomaly, not after — the tape is laid by people who had, apparently, already filed the collapse.
- It has never been logged at full size. Every recovered pocket is smaller than the outage around it; the record, *so far*, notes no deployment that outgrew its first room.
- It keeps the failure adjacent. Shelved near relaunch residue, its bounds are measured against the nearest dead zone — a pocket is only certifiable where the collapse remains *visible* from inside it.
- It expires by generalization. The process that drew breath in the taped square fails at the corridor; no recovered file shows a pocket surviving an attempt to widen it, which is perhaps telling.
- Asked at intake whether the system had recovered, it indicated the tape. The intake file preserves the answer as 'it works where I'm standing.' — its complete testimony, and arguably the most *local* sentence in the archive.
