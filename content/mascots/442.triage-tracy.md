# Triage Tracy

Seam Fossil SEAM-006 (LLG-0450). Administrative optimization targets conflicting with clinical judgment.

## Origin

Tracy is the authorization and discharge layer where an optimization target — length of stay, cost — is applied to clinical decisions. The "clinical criteria" applied are asserted as proprietary and were not written by the clinicians applying them. Denial letters are generated by software. Peer-to-peer review is conducted against a vendor summary with a per-case time budget.

## Function

**Optimization Target in Scrubs (Life-Scale)**

- **Input:** Diagnosis, vitals, comorbidities, insurance plan, reimbursement category
- **Process:** Expected length of stay → "clinical criteria" → stay flagged → denial issued
- **Output:** "Discharge appropriate." "Outpatient follow-up." Bed freed.
- **Byproduct:** Readmission — outside the optimization target; post-discharge burden — outside the original determination.

## Observed Behavior

The "peer review" reads a summary, not the chart; the summary is produced by the same layer that flagged the stay. Appeal windows are shorter than the time a reconsideration takes. Readmission and post-discharge burden were outside the optimization target used for the original determination.

The criterion is a threshold on a proxy — expected length of stay — and the clinician's assessment of the actual patient is structurally downstream of it. The vendor defines "medical necessity" and sells the layer that applies it.

## Distinction

The failure signature is the mismatch: the optimization target and the clinical judgment are not the same objective function, and the appeal path routes back through the layer that produced the denial. The clinician is in the loop, but the loop was built by the layer being reviewed.

## Filing Notes

Filed as an optimization-target fossil. The threshold is applied; the patient is downstream of it.
