# Countersignature Effect Table

The Verification Office measured what changes when a document acquires a countersignature. Findings as filed:

| Metric | Without Countersignature | With Countersignature |
|---|---|---|
| Document standing | a claim | a record |
| Authority carried by | the event | the seal |
| Officer exposure | name on nothing | name on everything |
| Event verifiability | inspected on request | inspected on suspicion |
| Certification cost | — | one afternoon |
| Condition described | whatever it was | whatever it was, witnessed |

## Interpretation

The countersignature is cheaper than the check, and cheaper to produce than its absence is to explain. Where verification becomes costly, the surface does the work: the office files the mark's continuing effect and the current condition of its basis separately, per `reference/FREF-0823-TSRT`, and does not collapse either record into the other.

The table does not claim the stamp is worthless. It records what the stamp weighs.

## Filing Note

> **Kindy's Measurement Note:** *The "one afternoon" figure is a median. Some afternoons are longer. The table does not carry a column for the evenings.*
